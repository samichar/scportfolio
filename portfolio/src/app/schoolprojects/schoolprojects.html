Thesis

For my thesis, I plan to build a machine learning model (possibly a convolution neural network) that predicts common defects, such as dislocations/strains/internal stresses, formed during the epitaxial growth stage of semiconductor fabrication. The model will take in parameters from a TCAD simulation I will be designing, which will be set up to match the real-world process as closely as possible. Since running real growth experiments is expensive and slow, the simulation functions as a surrogate, and the model will work out which growth parameters produce the fewest defects. This work is meant to support the University of Arkansas's new Multi-User Silicon Carbide (MUSiC) facility.

Reservoir Infrastructure for Threat Analysis (RITA, also known as MABEL)

MABEL is one demonstration of many in the Cybersecurity Consortium for Innovation (CCI) initiative through the RIOT Lab. It's a small-scale simulation of a pumped-energy storage system. Out in the real world, these systems pump water uphill to a reservoir when energy costs are cheap, then release it back down through a turbine when electricity is expensive, using gravity to turn that stored potential energy back into power. MABEL works the same way, just with tanks instead of mountains.

MABEL’s overall goal is to show how cyberattacks can disrupt power systems in a safe environment. For example, an attacker can spoof the water level sensor's readings, tricking the system into overfilling past the hard-set maximum tank value until water spills into the runoff tank, flooding the Lego town below.

  • Redid the system's wiring diagram, learning PLC programming and ladder logic along the way.
  • Created an HMI in Ignition Perspective, using Modbus TCP address bindings to connect it to the PLC. The HMI gives a user-friendly visual of what's happening in the system. It shows the upper and lower reservoirs with live water levels, lets users set maximum and minimum water levels, has buttons to fill/release/stop the system, and displays a scaled-up readout of the energy the turbine is producing.
  • Automated attack vectors in Python for MABEL and other demos, run from a virtual machine.
  • Presented MABEL at ShieldCon and the AIRES Kickoff.

Future work: The next step is making MABEL fully automated. Right now, someone has to manually press the buttons to fill and release the tank. The plan is to have the HMI track when energy costs are highest and lowest and have the system cycle on its own based on that, so attacks can be run without anyone having to operate the system at the same time.

Hackathon

Participated in Association for Computing Machinery’s 2025 Hackathon at the University of Arkansas, a 24-hour competition where teams compete to complete the best project aligning with the theme. The theme was "Yin and Yang," so the project needed two functions that were polar opposites but still complemented each other. I learned and taught GitHub management, HTML/CSS, JavaScript, and working with API endpoints.

We created a web-based app called Legal Action Role Play (LARP). The public side is a legal database, connected through an API, where you can search court cases and judges and learn about the Supreme Court. The hidden side is a role-play mode where you sign in as a character in a hypothetical court case. Hand-drawn art pulls you into the fake world, and if another user joins the same courtroom (you can pick the location), the two of you can argue back and forth, using what you learned from the database to play the part of a real lawyer.

LARP tied for 2nd place out of 36 teams and was awarded Most Creative Project.
